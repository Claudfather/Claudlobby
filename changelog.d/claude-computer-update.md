### Added — the preparation script reads, updates and continues a started computer

`host-install-v1.sh` has three more actions. `update-status` (operation `host-update-status-v1`) reports the receipt's revision, the installed revision, whether the receipt's revision is its ancestor, the binding, whether the computer is `untouched`, `in_use` or `started`, whether the system Python changed since preparation, and the update record with its kind (`update`, `start`, `first_team` or `addition`). `update` (`host-update-v1`) runs the installed `host update apply <target>` on a started computer, forward only and from the receipt's remote, and exits once the updater has dispatched. `update-continue` (`host-update-continue-v1`) runs `host update continue` held to the record it names. None waits for the updater, rewrites the receipt or asks a card. Each reads one canonical line with its own keys and answers in its own names; refusals are `installed_changed`, `update_in_progress`, `already_current`, `needs_reset`, `not_forward`, `no_such_request`, `nothing_to_continue`, `not_continuable` and `not_prepared`. A computer prepared but not started, untouched or partway through its cards, answers `needs_reset`. `prepare` and `status` are unchanged.

### Added — `host update continue` can be held to one request

The hidden flag `--expected-request <request_id>` makes `host update continue` refuse a record with another id, and exit 7 before any dispatch for a team preparation that was never dispatched: a Preview, a prepared first team or a prepared addition. Only its Launch dispatches it. A start, an ordinary update and any dispatched record are continued as before, a `done` record with health errors included. The flag is refused with any other action. Without it `host update continue` is unchanged.

### Changed — the update actions run in an environment of their own

The three actions build a fresh environment: the guide script's four pinned selectors, so a render reads this computer's settings and keeps its lasting owner service, and on Linux `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` and `DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS`, so the operator check and the cutover reach the account's user manager. They never set `PLANE_EMIT_DISABLED`. Tests pin each reason: without the settings variable the updater refuses the render because it would remove the lasting service, and without the user bus the operator check cannot verify its ancestry and refuses.
