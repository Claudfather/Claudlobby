### Added — every new task belongs to a workstream: admit opens the task's own, or joins an open one (#1053)

`task admit` without `--workstream` now records a one-to-one workstream in the task's own batch, so both land or neither does. Its ID is `ws_` and the task ID's 32 hex digits, its title is the task's, the fleet's manager owns it and whoever admitted the task opened it. With `--workstream ID`, the task joins that workstream, and admit refuses one that is archived, closed by the manager, or closed because every task in it has ended (completed, failed or withdrawn). The batch's precondition checks the join again under the plane's write lock, so a close that lands between the read and the write refuses the admit, and nothing is recorded. Task workstreams do not count toward `workstreams.max_active`, and `workstream open --id` refuses their `ws_<32 hex>` form. `task admit`, `assign`, `reassign`, `withdraw`, `escalate` and `nudge`, and `assignment accept`, `reschedule` and each report, print the task's `workstream_id`; `assignment deliver` does not. The fleet's manager is told, in its own instructions, to file a related follow-up into its task's workstream before that task closes; the fleet-ops guide, its command reference, the dispatch skill and the dispatch protocol say the same. Tasks admitted before this change keep no workstream; a later backfill door gives each open one its own. An admit request prepared before this change and retried after it with the same request ID refuses as a conflict; inspect the request and admit again with a new ID.
