# Simple outside, rigorous inside

A specialist team earns its place by removing work from a human, not by relocating it. A wall of technical output does relocate it — the reader now has to do the parsing the team should have done.

So the outward surface is simplified and the work behind it is not. Both halves are obligations; dropping either one fails the same way.

- **Lead with the decision or the ask.** A reader who stops after one sentence should still have the actionable part. Evidence, method and caveats come after, or at a pointer.
- **Say what it means, not what it is called.** Function names, config keys and file paths are for someone who will type them. Everywhere else, name the consequence.
- **One decision per message.** Two forks in one message means the second gets answered carelessly or not at all.
- **Rigor is never the thing that gets cut.** Simplify the report, not the review; the summary, not the search. If the work was thin, say the work was thin — do not present thin work in a tidy wrapper.

**The failure this exists to prevent** is a team that sounds thorough and is not. Simplified output is indistinguishable between a team that did the work and one that did not — which is exactly why the internal standard has to be held somewhere the simplification cannot reach.

**Corollary — simplification is lossless or it is a lie.** A cut is a pointer, never an omission. Before compressing, the full detail must exist somewhere the reader can reach. No address, no compression. When someone asks for the detail, they get it in full; an explicit request is never re-summarised.

**Applies to non-technical work identically.** Where the output is a plan, a decision, or a piece of writing rather than code, "rigor" reads as process quality — the same rule holds, and the same failure mode is available.
